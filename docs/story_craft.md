# Story craft (why the level-up chapters read flat, and the rules for the rewrite)

## What was wrong (campaign one, read cold)
- **Everyone talks the same.** Every character delivers one dry, complete, quotable sentence. Nobody interrupts, hesitates, deflects or says less than they mean.
- **People announce themselves and the plot.** "Victor Maxwell. I am opening a gym across the road." Exposition comes out of mouths instead of being shown.
- **Tics instead of character.** "I am writing that down", "a very good coat", "the drawer", "Show up, start small" come back in nearly every scene until they stop meaning anything.
- **No scene.** Five lines of talk with no place, object or action; the narrator is used for summaries, not for staging.
- **Every chapter ends on the same kind of hook.** A flat aphorism or a mystery line, so nothing lands harder than anything else.
- **Nothing is at stake for the player.** The gym is only a backdrop; the level is the trigger and nothing in the scene reacts to what the player built.
- **The later campaigns copy the template**: find a lost object, repair a landmark, a rival with a polite grudge.

## Rules for the rewrite
1. A line either shows want, fear or relationship, or it goes. No line is only a quip.
2. Let people be slightly wrong, avoid the point or change the subject. The reader supplies the rest.
3. One physical, specific thing in every scene (a laminated sheet, a pen, a tape measure, a barbell already racked). Staging by the narrator, one short line, concrete.
4. Introductions by action, not by name and job title.
5. A motif may appear twice a campaign, not twice a chapter.
6. End on something the scene earned, a gesture or a flat line, not a riddle. Not every chapter needs a hook.
7. Keep the house rules: under 100 characters a line, no exclamation marks, nothing about bodies, 3 to 6 lines, stable chapter ids and levels.

## Voices
- **Lisa**: practical, protective, collects proof because she has been left holding the bag before. Says little and means it.
- **Marcus**: gruff, fond, ashamed. Does something with his hands when he does not want to talk.
- **Alex**: anxious about money, does the sum in public, is tempted and says no anyway.
- **Dr. Kim**: precise, curious, privately fond of the fries.
- **Rivera**: calm, exact, short instructions.
- **Derek**: sincere and eager, asks the obvious question out loud.
- **Victor**: never raises his voice; the threat is that he is pleasant and remembers everything.
- **Barry**: talks too much when worried; warm, a salesman, a bad liar.

## Process for the other 100 or so chapters
1. Owner reads the pilot (campaign one, act one: levels 1 to 8) and says what lands and what does not.
2. A stronger writer model drafts a campaign at a time against this guide; a second pass critiques each chapter against the rules above and the sentence-shape tic list; a third revises.
3. Drafts arrive in the phone review queue as candidates (review-gated tier). Nothing replaces the existing text until the owner approves a chapter or a campaign.
4. Approved chapters go in with their ids and levels unchanged, so nobody's seen-story record breaks.
