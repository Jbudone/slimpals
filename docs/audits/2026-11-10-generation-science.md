# Audit 7: generation science (2026-11-10)

From a Fable subagent briefed as an AI researcher on creative text and humour. [V] = verified in the repo, [L] = literature recalled from memory (check before citing), [I] = inference.

## Why the text is flat, ranked
1. No premise and no speakers [V]: `writerPrompt` (`server/services/content/writersRoom.ts`) opens "Two regulars at a small neighbourhood gym talk for ten seconds." Input is a bare situation and anonymous A/B; `Banter` has no speaker field. Nothing has happened, so the model can only remark on a gym.
2. The target style is the model's default register [V]: `npc_banter.md` (dry, weary, under 12 words, flattest last line, no exclamation marks) while the cast was designed louder (Tom shouts, Lisa "Hi hi!"). One register + one ending rule = one joke shape.
3. Device-first generation is backwards [V/I]: devices (`comedy.ts`) deliver an idea, they do not supply one. Four of ten device examples are the same lines as `GOLD_BANTER`, which is also the judge's anchors and the bake-off's "hand-written" arm: one small agent-written set steers generation, judging and the baseline.
4. Selection does not measure funny [V]: the judge rubric (`judge.ts`) scores specific, dry, natural, in_character (no funny dimension) on flash-lite; `shortlist()` filters only `funny >= 2` and never uses the parsed `turnWorks`/`generic`. Best-of-N against it selects typical and tidy.
5. Critique-then-revise smooths [V/I]. 6. No temperature or top_p passed (`modelClient.ts`), one call per writer per situation [V].
7. Small bugs [V]: `hasCliche` matches substrings, so "gains" inside "against" silently drops any exchange containing "against"; the `bro` lines use "Let's go"/"gains", which the filter bans; the default writer slug is unverified.
8. The model itself, last [L]: LLMs repeat a small set of jokes (Jentzsch and Kersting 2023), comedians find output bland (Mirowski 2024), GPT jokes rate about level with laypeople (Gorenz and Schwarz 2024), models rank humour poorly (Hessel 2023), preference tuning loses diversity (Kirk 2023, Padmakumar and He 2023). Realistic ceiling: "funny friend on an average day", and only with a good premise.
Why the writers' room samples failed [I]: the "engines" are catchphrases (funny only once you love the character); they lean on lore invented in the same document; they are compressed past sense under the word cap; gag ladders came before any kept line. Cards and ledgers should be extracted from lines the owner kept, not prescribed. Drop audit 1's "templated fragments with slots" arm (it reproduces the catchphrase failure).

## Recipe
Seed (owner, one sentence) -> premises, separate from dialogue (8 one-sentence "games" from two model families at temperature 1.0, each with an obviousness score, exactly two 5s so the obvious ones can be avoided; the owner taps one or two, a 2-second choke point) -> drafts (six takes as separate calls across two models, speakers and retrieved kept lines, no devices or style adjectives, written long then cut) ->
mechanical gates only (length, body talk, near-duplicate, word-boundary cliche matcher; no LLM funny score; one optional cold-read sense check by another model that only flags "may not make sense") -> owner keeps/edits/kills with line locks -> retrieval only: up to 8 kept exchanges (same speakers first), 3-5 before->after edit pairs, 3 kills with reasons. No fine-tuning below ~300-500 kept items (probably never needed: ~60-90 exchanges heard per month).
A calibrated judge only after ~150 owner verdicts, to order the queue, never to gate. Agrees with the tools engineer's page (seed, six takes, edit/lock, retrieval), with two changes: show each take's one-line game (or pick the premise first) and generate takes as parallel calls across two models. Bro Hype and D&D are the same recipe at scene scale (the owner approves the beat sheet, not the script).

## Prompts (full text and example runs are in the report transcript; summary)
Template 1 premises: "8 different games ... the one unusual thing a person does or believes, followed by 'so...' and what that logic leads to next ... obviousness 1-5, exactly two 5s". Template 2 dialogue from a premise: line 1 makes the situation clear to a stranger; the odd person stays sincere; push one step further each line; stop on the furthest step; 4-6 lines up to 18 words; talk like people; kept/edit/kill/locked sections.
Template 3 punch-up: GAME (or "no game" and the nearest one), CUT everything after the biggest laugh, SENSE (fix by adding words), three BUTTONS that each do something different, one alternative SWING with a different game. Example run: seed "Tom dry-scoops pre-workout because a guy online said water dilutes the intent" gave games like "Tom has gone dry on everything: oats, shakes, the shower" and a scene ending "Kim: And the dry shower? Tom: Quick."

## Experiment (no new infrastructure)
Owner writes 4 one-sentence seeds; same named pairs in every arm. Arms: A current writers' room; B one strong model, single shot; C seed -> six takes directly; D seed -> owner-picked premise -> takes; E 12 existing scripted exchanges from `bonds.ts`/`banter.ts` (the bar already in the app). 12 items per arm (60 cards), shuffled, arm hidden, one tap each: Keep / Fix / No, plus optional "Huh?" (does not make sense).
Rule: usable = Keep + Fix out of 12. A method works at >=5 usable, >=2 Keep, no more than 1 behind arm E. Adopt D over C only if it beats C by 3+ usable. If B is within 1 of the best arm the method is not the lever: simplify and spend on the model. More than 3 of 12 "Huh?" means compression or lore, lengthen lines first.
Abandon AI banter generation if the best AI arm scores 2 or fewer usable of 12 and one repeat with a different top model also scores 2 or fewer. Regardless: freeze the unattended nightly banter job. Owner time: seeds 3 min, premise picks 2 min, rating ~10 min. 12 per arm detects only large differences (4 of 12 has a ~10-65% interval).
Small fixes to ship regardless: word-boundary cliche matcher, use `turnWorks`/`generic` in `shortlist`, a temperature option in `modelClient.ts`.
