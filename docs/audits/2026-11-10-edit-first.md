# Audit 9: edit-first comedy, Bro Hype and Friday D&D (2026-11-10)

From an Opus subagent briefed as a satirical-news editor-in-chief.

- **Verified in the repo:** `RoomLine` is `{who, text}`; `SPEAKERS` has no Brody, Tom, other bros or crowd; the `fad` arc ends on a narrator summary; the `bro` coach block is stock.
- **Checked on the web:** one search confirmed 12-3-30, Japanese interval walking, rebounding, 75 Hard and "becoming Chinese" warm water as current.
- **From memory (unverified):** the other fads.

## Why the samples failed

- **Tics, not premises.** A habit with no situation is a catchphrase.
- **Private logic.** "I've given it a spreadsheet" and "Best I've felt about one" do not parse in one read.
- **No headline first.** A premise must be funny as one sentence before anyone writes dialogue.

The fix is the order of work: headline, beats, draft, punch-up. The owner's time goes on kills, not rewrites.

## The pipeline (the owner is editor-in-chief)

| Step | Who | Owner time |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Pitch | Claude writes 60 headlines, cuts to the best 20 against the rubric | 0 |
| 2. Kill round | Owner replies `keep 3 7 12`; anything not named is dead | about 2 min per 20 |
| 3. Beats | Five one-line beats per kept headline, button stated | 0-1 min |
| 4. Draft | 30% over length | 0 |
| 5. Punch-up | Copy chief applies named moves and shows BEFORE / AFTER / MOVE | 0 |
| 6. Continuity | Fact checker | 0 |
| 7. Read-through | Owner replies `strike 4 9, button B` | 2-3 min |
| 8. Ship | PR, plus two ledger lines (what happened, what is now true) | 0 |

The fact checker reports only: a contradiction, a speaker missing from `SPEAKERS`, a line over 100 characters, body or weight talk, a gag advanced more than one step, or a character speaking out of card.

### The ten punch-up moves

1. **Kill the narrator button.**
2. **Cut the restatement.**
3. **Make the callback the button.**
4. **Swap the safe noun for the weirder true noun, and put a person in it.**
5. **Use fake precision.**
6. **Give the straight man a short, plain line that sets the trap.**
7. **Raise the stakes of the dumb thing until it has a consequence in the world.**
8. **Make it make sense in one read.**
9. **One joke per line; drop the hedge.**
10. **Use contractions and spoken rhythm.**

### Worked example: from topic to headline

1. "Gym Bros Try Red Light Therapy": a topic, not a joke. Killed.
2. "Bro's Red Light Mask Makes Him Look Like A Tomato": about how he looks, no consequence. Killed.
3. "Bro In Red Light Mask Mistaken For Fire Exit Sign; Fourteen Members Follow Him Outside": pictureable, a consequence, could only happen in this gym. Kept.

## Bro Hype

### The bros

- **Brody, the pioneer.** Believes trying something first makes him its discoverer. Sincere, easily moved, never mean.
- **Kev, the entrepreneur.** Every fad is a product he is already selling. It survives being debunked.
- **Theo, the sceptic.** Correct about everything, then converts for a reason that has nothing to do with the evidence.
- **Dale, "since 2019".** Has done it longer than it has existed. When caught, retreats to an older, worse version ("with a lamp").
- **Chad and Kyle** are rare guests.

### Format (12-20 lines, about 40 seconds)

1. Cold open, already mid-fad.
2. The pitch: a fake-precise claim and Kev's price.
3. Theo's straight line, and Dale's claim.
4. The demonstration goes wrong in the world (other people, staff, the building), never on a body.
5. The converted sceptic.
6. The button belongs to a character. Brody may have the last word when he is sincere.

### The fad generator (52 a year)

- 26 real trends parodied and 26 invented, refreshed monthly by web search.
- Eight twist operators, one per headline: take the claim literally; apply it to the wrong thing; "-maxxing" past sense; Kev monetises it; the ritual outlives the reason; the measurement replaces the activity; invented ancient history; an absurdly precise protocol.
- Template: "[Bro] [fad], [consequence]".
- House rule: no fads about appearance or weight. Performance, sleep, gadgets and supplements only.

## The 5-second rubric

### Headline (kill it if it fails either of the first two)

1. Can I picture it in one read?
2. Is the second half a consequence I did not see coming, not a description?
3. Is the target a fad or a character's delusion, never a body?
4. Would I say it out loud to a friend?

### Line (one "no" means edit, two means cut)

1. Did I understand it first time, without lore?
2. Does it add something (a turn, new information)?
3. If I cover the name, do I know who said it?
4. Is the funniest word last?
5. Could it be shorter?

### The AI never decides these alone

- Keep or kill on any headline.
- The final button.
- Anything that becomes canon (running gags, ledger facts).
- Any line about a real friend.
- Voice-card changes.
- What reaches players.

No AI judge gates humour.

### Collaboration tool

Start in chat with the reply grammar (`keep`, `strike`, `button B`). If the rhythm sticks, build a small `/desk` page:

- a headline board you swipe to keep or kill;
- a scene view where you tap a line to strike it, ask for three alternatives, or type your own;
- a canon list.

Verdicts go in the database, read through one export endpoint. Build it only after two chat rounds have worked.

## First exercise (owner, about 10 minutes)

1. Reply with the numbers to keep from the 20 Bro Hype and 10 D&D headlines.
2. Read "The Glow" and "The Prophecy" and reply `strike N` for any failing line.
3. Claude then takes the two best keeps through the full pipeline (about 3 more minutes of reading).

**It worked** if the owner keeps at least 5 of 20 headlines and ships a scene with two strikes or fewer (better: a friend quotes a line).
**If fewer than 3 of 20 survive,** the premises are broken, not the dialogue. The owner writes five headlines and Claude executes them.
