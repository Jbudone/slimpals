# Audit 5: improv and sketch craft (2026-11-10)

From an Opus subagent briefed as a veteran improv teacher and sitcom writer, after the owner read the writers' room samples and said none seemed funny or even made sense.
(Four more experts started the same day, an AI tools engineer, a generation researcher, a systemic narrative designer and a satirical editor, but hit the usage limit; only this one finished.)

## Why the samples failed
- Earlier audits blamed a missing voice. The deeper problem: the samples swap one template (dry deflation) for six (Marcus says a number, Lisa says "Noted. Under X", Derek says at most three words). That is catchphrase Mad Libs, the most recognisable AI-comedy smell.
  A catchphrase only lands once the audience already loves the character, and nobody has watched these people do anything.
- Samples lean on lore that lives only in the cards ("looming", "locker 14", "Barry's bag"), so "some don't even make sense" is accurate. Banter bubbles are glimpsed mid-action, so a joke needing three inferential steps dies there.
- Verdict on the 12: 2 work (#7 Marcus out-counted by Derek; #10 Rivera "second: also you"), 3 half work (#1, #8, #11), 7 fail. #12 is warmth, not comedy.
- Cards: Tom is the best (want collides with behaviour); Kim (hypocrite expert) and Rivera (contest from nothing) are good premises; Marcus needs a heart; Lisa "files things" is a tic, her real trait is that she knows things she shouldn't;
  Derek "three words or fewer" is a constraint, not a person. Biggest miss: all six are eccentrics and there is no straight man. Make Jordan the permanent straight man.
- Repairs: Kim "Fries after a workout undo the whole workout." Derek "You're eating fries." Kim "I haven't worked out." / Lisa "Every other locker is free, Derek." Derek "Fourteen." Lisa (to Jordan) "It's been broken since March. He kicks it open every day. It's a relationship." /
  Kim "Eight glasses a day? Myth." Marcus "Finally. I'm on six." Kim "It's nine."

## Mining the real friend group (15 minutes, voice memo or fast bullets; habits, opinions, objects and stories, never bodies)
1. What does [friend] say they'll do at the gym, and what do they actually do? 2. The stupid argument this group has had five times. 3. The lie everyone tells and everyone knows is a lie. 4. What does [friend] do that nobody mentions anymore? 5. Who is most competitive about the least important thing, and what is it?
6. An item [friend] always carries that says everything. 7. The group's oldest running joke and the real incident behind it. 8. Who always has a new thing, and the last three. 9. What does each friend say when about to quit something (exact words)? 10. The responsible one, and the one area where they are a mess.
11. Something someone did once that the group will never let go. 12. Each friend's gym nickname if the others picked it.
Claude then finds the gap (claim vs behaviour), makes it a want and a flaw, and decides which character carries it. Worked example: "Sam logs a dog walk as cardio; the dog is fourteen and sat down outside the bakery" becomes a 10-line scene where Sam keeps reframing weak facts
as stronger training ("Interval training. He sets the intervals." "Weighted carry. Even better. Put it under strength.") and Lisa's closer lands because it is earned: "Biscuit is also logging forty minutes. He's ahead of you this week."

## Game of the scene (20-second cutscenes, 6-10 lines)
The game is the first unusual thing, played as a repeatable pattern: "[Person] treats [X] as if it were [Y]". Establish by line 3, heighten 2-3 times (each bigger), keep a straight man, end with the game winning or looping on itself, never a moral or summary.
Five ways AI sketches die, and the fix: a situation with no game (write the "treats X as Y" sentence or cut); the joke explained or a narrator summary at the end (delete everything after the laugh; narrators may set scenes, never end them); the resolution reflex of kindness (the last line leaves someone still wrong);
same beat at the same size (each repeat must escalate or reverse, or be cut); lore and abstraction instead of a picture (cold-reader test: could someone who opened the app today picture it?).

## Bro Hype
A weekly cutscene where the bros adopt a ridiculous fad with total sincerity until reality stops them, then pivot to next week's fad, born from this week's accident. Bros, each with a delusion: Brody (the pioneer: believes he discovered it, never mean), Kev (the entrepreneur: merch within a minute),
Theo (the sceptic who folds hardest), Dale (got there first: "been doing it since 2019"); straight men Dr. Kim (asked to endorse everything) and Marcus (counts the damage). Five beats in 8-12 lines: unveil with a fake origin; adoption; overcommit; collision; pivot button.
Fad supply: a real wellness trend taken one step too literally and attached to an ordinary gym object, rotating category (supplement, diet rule, exercise trick, gadget, ritual); the owner drops one real fad a week; a fad ledger prevents repeats. Seeds: mouth taping; intermittent lifting; grounding; cold exposure for the coffee; "seed oils" (Brody avoids tournament seeding);

## Friday night D&D
Funny when the people leak into the fiction: epic register against petty office reality, workplace grudges fought through characters, the DM abusing power, real interruptions. Each Friday one real thing from the week (the owner gives one line) becomes the fight, the group's check-ins are the d20 (high/mid/low variants), an absent friend is "guarding the horses".
Aim for 12 lines, one game, one roll (the earlier 40-line premiere is a mini-film). ## Recommended first exercise (about 25 owner minutes, no new tooling)
The owner answers the 12 questions in a voice memo or text dump and pastes them to Claude. Claude writes three short scenes (a friend scene, a Bro Hype, a D&D) and ten banter exchanges, each built on something the owner said and tagged with its source. The owner marks each k/x/edit (~10 minutes).
It worked if: at least 3 of 10 exchanges and 1 scene are kept untouched (the writers' room samples kept 2 of 12), and a friend reacts in the group chat; a correction ("it was TWO blocks") is the best result because it is free new material. If neither happens the problem is not the model or format. Until this passes: no judge, bake-off or story council.
The collaborative tool starts as exactly this chat ritual; later a page with three columns per line: source, line, k/x/edit.
